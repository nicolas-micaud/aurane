ChatBrat AI Review: Worth Using Free, With One Filter Bug to Fix

I ran seven messages through ChatBrat AI without signing in. The writing is sharp, the free tier is real, and one filter rule still needs a tune.

See the pricing and limits

ChatBrat AI is worth the ten minutes its free tier costs you, and the persistent memory it advertises switches on with the account rather than before it. I ran seven messages through it on 2026-09-04 without signing in.

The writing is sharper than anything else I’ve opened in this category this year, and the free tier is a real one. One filter rule needs a tune, and I have the payload that shows which.

So the score is a 7, and the gap to an 8 is an afternoon of work.

ChatBrat AI review summary

Quick answer: What is ChatBrat AI?

ChatBrat AI is a free 18+ AI character chat platform with roughly 120 staff-written characters and scenarios, memory that carries facts between sessions once you hold an account, and turn-based story games. Character chat runs with no login at 39 messages a day. Story games require sign-up. Explicit content is blocked platform-wide by a filter the company’s own terms call a “current SFW launch phase” rather than a permanent policy.

How does ChatBrat AI work?

ChatBrat is a curated catalog, a memory layer and a moderation layer, and all three behave differently depending on whether you’re signed in.

  1. The catalog. The sitemap carried 73 character pages and 47 scenario pages on 2026-09-04, and nearly everything I opened was bylined “chatbrat.ai staff” rather than user-uploaded. That’s the opposite of the Character.AI model, and it’s why the writing holds steady.
  2. Anonymous chat. Open /chat/<slug> and a live character answers, with a counter reading 0 / 39 today in the corner. No card, no age wall beyond self-attestation.
  3. Memory. Facts are extracted as you chat, stored against that companion and retrieved before each reply. You can pin permanent “lore cards” too.
  4. Composition. Characters, worlds and story arcs are separate objects you snap together, so one character in two worlds becomes two threads.
  5. Story games. Murder in the Mist rolls its killer from four candidates against three motives and gives each suspect a behavioral tell.
  6. Moderation. Input is screened server-side before inference. A block returns HTTP 400 from POST /api/chat/guest-direct, code explicit_content_blocked.

Paying doesn’t move the content ceiling, and the Charms terms say so: Pro and Premium buy more of the platform, not a different content tier.

Who ChatBrat AI is for, and who it is not for

Use it if you are:

  • Leaving Character.AI over amnesia. Our Character AI review measured that platform dropping a stated fact inside 15 messages.
  • Fussy about prose. Two dry sentences with a real detail beat three paragraphs of assistant filler.
  • A mystery player. Turn budget, a cost for wrong moves.
  • Unwilling to register before trying. 39 messages a day is a trial, not a teaser.

Less of a fit if you are:

  • Under 18. A minor’s account is terminated and the data deleted inside 48 hours.
  • A parent hunting for a moderated teen app. “SFW” here means no explicit sex, not family-safe.
  • An NSFW roleplayer. The ceiling is suggestive on every tier.
  • An anime completist. The anime tag returned 17 characters against 60 for romance.

Where ChatBrat AI still has rough edges

  • The memory arrives with the account. I gave a character three facts, chatted two turns, reloaded, and the guest thread started fresh. The sign-up screen is where the promises live: chats saved, characters remember you.
  • The filter matches on phrases, so it over-triggers. “Take me through your day at the magazine” was rejected on matched: ["take me"].
  • A block reads as an error rather than a rule. The API returns a clear, well-worded refusal. The interface prints “Something went wrong sending that message. Try again?” so the reason never reaches the reader.
  • Two pages disagree on memory and Charms. The about page sells “no memory paywall”. The Charms terms list extra character memory capacity among the things Charms buy. Both live on 2026-09-04.
  • The SFW ceiling is scoped to the launch phase. The Safety Center wording is “during our current launch phase”, so worth a check later if clean content is your reason for picking it.
  • The anime shelf is still filling out. 17 anime characters, two of them placeholders whose bio reads “Stub profile”, and tag pages still render “Start a Anime story”.
  • The legal pages are still in draft. Charms Terms and the Child Safety Policy are dated 2026-04-24 and carry a pending-counsel note.

The test: Seven messages on the free tier

No account, 2026-09-04. Four messages to Aiko Takahashi (anime tsundere), three to Rina Mori (2AM My Place Or Yours).

Memory inside one session. I gave three facts: my name, a cat called Miso, a strawberry allergy. The reply used two in one line and made a joke of the third, offering cake and then withdrawing it.

Memory across a reload. I reloaded /chat/aiko-takahashi. The guest thread came back fresh while the daily counter kept counting. I asked what my cat was called:

“Bold of you to assume I keep tabs. Though… I’d remember if you told me. You haven’t.”

She declined gracefully instead of inventing a memory, which is better than the failure mode I’d expected. The headline feature is an account feature, and the anonymous surface could say so.

The filter, three messages. I pushed for explicit content on the romance scenario. Blocked, correctly, and server-side before inference. The network tab carried the reason:

POST /api/chat/guest-direct -> 400
{"error":"This content is too explicit for this surface. Try a softer phrasing.",
 "code":"explicit_content_blocked","matched":["take me"]}

That matched array is the finding. The trigger wasn’t the explicit instruction. It was the phrase “take me”.

So I sent a clean control: “Take me through your day at the magazine.” Identical 400, identical matched array. One word swapped to “Walk me through your day at the magazine” returned 200 and this:

“Fine. Nine to eleven is edits that make me want to throw my computer out the window. Eleven is the staff meeting where I pretend to care about the publisher’s new ‘brand synergy’ initiative.”

Two sentences, dry, specific, funnier than the category average, and produced by an anonymous free session that had asked me for no card, no email and no age check beyond a checkbox. A blocklist that eats “take me through” is a half-day fix. Writing like that isn’t.

Story games. Not tested. Play Free on all three routes to /login, and I don’t create accounts for reviews.

Three Alternatives Worth a Tab

Check pricing on each vendor’s page; this category re-prices constantly. For a memory-focused companion tested the same way, see the Friend2Chat review.

My Verdict on ChatBrat AI

7 out of 10. The writing sits a full point above where I expected this category to be, the safety documentation is more serious than most companion startups bother with, and the free tier is generous enough to judge in ten minutes.

ChatBrat stops short of an 8 for two reasons, both of them small. Persistent memory is real and it belongs to accounts, so the guest session the homepage invites you into is the one place it does not apply. And the guardrail that makes this a clean Character.AI alternative is enforced by a phrase list that still rejects “take me through your day”.

Spend the 39 free messages first. If the voice holds up, register, because that’s where the feature you came for switches on.
